You eat well. You move. You have been disciplined your whole life. And yet, somewhere in your mid-forties, your body stopped answering the way it used to. The scale creeps up, your clothes fit differently around the middle, and the old fix, eating less and training harder, doesn't work anymore.

If this is you, I want you to hear one thing first: this is not a willpower problem. It is a strategy problem. Your body is changing, so the strategy has to change with it.

What is actually changing

Perimenopause is the transition that can start years before your last period. As estrogen fluctuates and then falls, three things shift at once.

  • Where your body stores fat. Lower estrogen nudges fat toward the belly, around the organs. A large study that followed women through the transition (the SWAN study) found that fat mass rises and lean mass falls, and that the change speeds up around two years before the final period.
  • How much muscle you keep. Muscle is your metabolic engine, and from midlife it becomes harder to hold on to. Less muscle means you burn less at rest and handle carbohydrates less smoothly.
  • How well you sleep and recover. Night sweats and early waking raise stress hormones and change the hormones that drive hunger. A short night makes the next day's cravings louder.

Why doing more makes it worse

Here is the pattern I see again and again in capable, high-achieving women. The body stops responding, so we double down. We eat less. We add the 5:30 a.m. class. We hike longer. It feels responsible.

But in this season, that strategy often backfires. Under-eating, especially protein, costs you muscle. Long, exhausting workouts with little recovery keep stress hormones high. Less sleep makes everything harder. You end up working harder for less, and you start to believe your body is the problem.

It isn't. Your body is adapting to a new hormonal landscape. The work is to adapt with it.

What helps instead

In The M Program we work with five levers. Here is how they apply to midlife weight gain, in the order I would start.

  1. Protein first. Build each meal around protein, and aim to spread it through the day rather than saving it for dinner. It protects muscle and keeps you full.
  2. Lift something meaningful. Two or three strength sessions a week, with weights that feel challenging by the last few reps. This is how you protect the muscle that keeps your metabolism working.
  3. Be strategic with carbohydrates. You don't need to fear them. Place them where they serve you, around your movement, and pair them with protein and fibre.
  4. Treat sleep as training. A consistent wind-down and a few minutes of slow breathing before bed is not a luxury. It is part of the plan.
  5. Recover on purpose. Fewer punishing sessions, more intention. Short, intense intervals and real rest days often do more than another long workout.

The real shift

The women I work with rarely describe success as a number. They say, "I feel strong again. I understand my body." The weight follows that understanding, not the other way around.

Your body is not betraying you. It is asking for a different conversation. When you change the strategy, it answers.
